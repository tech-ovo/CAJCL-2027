# Regulatory Compliance & Data Governance Reference (ID-Only Student Model)

**Platform:** 72nd Annual CAJCL State Convention Platform (`state.uhsjcl.org`)  
**Operator:** California Junior Classical League (CAJCL), 501(c)(3) Non-Profit

Student names and parent emergency contacts reside solely on physical paper waiver/medical forms and on a private Google Spreadsheet kept exclusively by each Chapter Sponsor (never shared with CAJCL or the website).

## 1. Executive Summary: How the ID-Only Model Transforms Compliance

By replacing student first and last names with a **CAJCL-assigned Delegate ID**, removing chapter join codes (`#/join`), and maintaining the platform's strict ban on student email addresses, home addresses, dates of birth, SSNs, payment card data, and digital health records, the web database holds **zero direct personal identifiers for students**.

Under federal and California privacy statutes, this architectural choice shifts student records from **Personally Identifiable Information (PII)** to **de-identified / pseudonymous operational records**:
1. **Eliminates Pre-Consent Student Access Under COPPA:** Because chapter join codes have been removed, students cannot create accounts before a parent signs the physical waiver. An Assigned Delegate ID, grade (6–12), Latin level, and hashed login token (`DEL-XXXXX-XXXXX`) distributed by the sponsor after collecting the signed physical waiver do not constitute personal information under 16 C.F.R. § 312.2 (and fall within COPPA's "support for internal operations" exception).
2. **Satisfies FERPA's De-Identified Record Standard:** Under 34 C.F.R. § 99.31(b)(1), teachers (sponsors) may share de-identified student records coded with an assigned ID without triggering FERPA disclosure restrictions, because the sponsor keeps the private Google Spreadsheet mapping `Assigned Delegate ID ↔ Student Legal Name` strictly at the school level and never shares it with CAJCL or the platform.
3. **Neutralizes Statutory Data Breach Triggers for Student DB Records:** California's breach notification statute (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.82) defines covered "personal information" around an individual's **first name or first initial and last name** combined with sensitive elements or account credentials. Without student names in the database, a database incident cannot expose student names paired with any statutory element.

## 2. Regulatory Compliance Narrative (ID-Only Student Architecture)

### COPPA *(15 U.S.C. §§ 6501–6506 and 16 C.F.R. Part 312 — Children's Online Privacy Protection Act)*
Although 501(c)(3) non-profit organizations are generally exempt from COPPA under Section 5 of the FTC Act, we voluntarily adhere to COPPA's safeguards because middle school delegates include students under the age of 13. COPPA requires operators to post a clear online privacy notice (16 C.F.R. § 312.4), obtain verifiable parental or school-official consent before collecting personal information from children under 13 (§ 312.5), allow parents to review and delete their child's records (§ 312.6), minimize data collection (§ 312.7), maintain reasonable security (§ 312.8), and retain data only as long as necessary (§ 312.10).

Under our ID-only architecture, the platform collects zero student names, email addresses, phone numbers, home addresses, or birth dates. We have also eliminated chapter join codes (`#/join`) so that students cannot create accounts on the website before a parent signs the physical waiver. Instead, students are represented exclusively by a sequential or randomly generated Assigned Delegate ID and a hashed `DEL-XXXXX-XXXXX` login token used strictly for internal convention operations (16 C.F.R. § 312.2). If a sponsor allows students to log in and fill out their own activity sheets, those login tokens are distributed only after parents have signed the physical permission and waiver forms.

The only feature where COPPA-covered personal information could arise is the optional at-convention Photo Contest, since a photograph showing a child's face falls within COPPA's definition of personal information. We address this in three ways. First, the physical *Student Waiver & Permission Form* signed by parents before convention includes a media and photography release covering convention photography. Second, we strip all embedded metadata from uploaded photos in two stages: the student's browser (`frontend/public/js/pages/photos.js`) re-draws each image onto an HTML5 canvas to produce a resized JPEG (up to 2400 pixels) and thumbnail (480 pixels), which removes phone location, camera, and timestamp data before upload; then our backend server (`backend/lib/photos.py`) verifies the file's magic bytes and strips JPEG `APP1` (EXIF and XMP GPS/camera data), `APP13` (IPTC blocks), and `COM` (comment) segments, as well as PNG `eXIf`, `tEXt`, `iTXt`, `zTXt`, and `tIME` chunks, rejecting any malformed file that cannot be cleanly parsed. (Embedded images in Google Docs site pages undergo the same server-side metadata stripping in `scripts/build_pages.py`.) Stored photo files in Google Drive are named solely by Chapter and Assigned Delegate ID rather than student names. Third, parents or sponsors can redact a delegate's record (`roster.redact`) or withdraw a photo at any time, which immediately deletes their selections, sessions, and Drive uploads.

### SOPIPA *(Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 22584 — Student Online Personal Information Protection Act)*
SOPIPA applies mandatorily to online platforms designed and used primarily for K–12 school purposes, with no exemption for 501(c)(3) non-profits. It prohibits targeted advertising, commercial profiling of students, and the sale of student data (§ 22584(b)); requires reasonable security procedures (§ 22584(d)(1)) and deletion of student data under school control (§ 22584(d)(2)); and requires binding third-party service providers contractually (§ 22584(b)(4)(E)). However, Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 22584(g) explicitly exempts de-identified student information once all direct identifiers are removed.

Our core database records (Assigned Delegate ID, Chapter, Grade 6–12, Latin Level, and test or activity selections) are de-identified from the moment of collection. The platform carries zero advertisements, tracking pixels, or commercial analytics, and all fonts (`.woff2` subsets) and scripts are self-hosted so browsers never make external requests to third-party trackers. Pre-convention creative contests are judged on a strictly double-blind basis where judges see only `Entry N` with original filenames and chapter names stripped, and the Certamen practice arena leaderboard aggregates scores strictly by chapter rather than individual usernames. All uploaded contest files, photo contest images, and any digital records are permanently purged 30 days after convention on **April 12, 2027**, and rolling database backups in Google Drive are deleted after **10 days**.

### FERPA *(20 U.S.C. § 1232g and 34 C.F.R. Part 99)* & *Cal. Educ. Code § 49073*
FERPA and California Education Code § 49073 protect the privacy of student education records held by schools and prohibit unauthorized disclosure of personally identifiable student information. Crucially, under FERPA's de-identified data safe harbor (34 C.F.R. § 99.31(b)(1)), school officials (chapter sponsors) may release student records without triggering FERPA disclosure restrictions when all personally identifiable information has been removed and replaced with a school-held record code.

To comply with this standard, the web platform stores only the Assigned Delegate ID, while each chapter sponsor maintains a separate Google Spreadsheet (modeled on our Sample Sponsor Spreadsheet) that maps each Assigned Delegate ID to the student's legal name. **That Google Spreadsheet is kept privately by the sponsor and is never shared with CAJCL, convention organizers, or the website**, which prevents re-identification of any database record on our platform. Furthermore, Assigned Delegate IDs and login tokens are generated sequentially or via cryptographically secure random number generation—never derived from student names, initials, Social Security numbers, or school district ID numbers. Every API endpoint enforces role-based access control (`test_endpoints.py`), isolating each chapter's records to its own `school_id` so sponsors can view only their own chapter.

### AB 1584 *(Cal. Educ. Code § 49073.1 — Local Educational Agency Digital Contracts)*
AB 1584 governs contracts between California public school districts (Local Educational Agencies, or LEAs) and third-party digital operators managing personally identifiable "pupil records." The statute requires contract terms affirming that pupil records remain the property of the LEA, explaining how parents and students can review and correct data, limiting data use to specified educational purposes, requiring staff training and breach notification, and certifying post-term deletion. However, Cal. Educ. Code § 49073.1(d)(2) expressly excludes de-identified information from the definition of "pupil records."

Because no student names or contact details are entered into our database and the sponsor's re-identification spreadsheet is never shared with us, AB 1584 is not strictly triggered by our web database. Nevertheless, we include the full statutory School District Addendum in our Privacy Policy so that any participating school district IT office requiring standard AB 1584 contractual language has it automatically in place. Under this policy, parents and students can review or correct any delegate record through their Chapter Sponsor or the Host School Sponsor. While registration forms have a soft deadline of **February 13, 2027**, sponsors can continue to edit delegate records and correct mistakes after that date.

### California Reasonable Security Law *(Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.81.5)*
California Civil Code § 1798.81.5 requires organizations that own, license, or maintain "personal information" about California residents—defined in § 1798.81.5(d)(1) as an individual's first name or first initial and last name combined with sensitive elements or login credentials—to implement and maintain reasonable security procedures and practices, and to require the same of third-party service providers by contract.

Because student database rows contain no names, they fall outside the statutory definition of personal information; however, adult sponsor, chaperone, and board member records (which include adult names, email addresses, and phone numbers) as well as any physical waiver scans remain covered. We protect the platform through comprehensive technical safeguards: mandatory TLS 1.3 encryption in transit; AES-256 volume encryption at rest on Turso; zero cleartext credentials (`HMAC-SHA256` with an isolated secret pepper for login tokens and client IP addresses, and `SHA-256` for 256-bit session tokens); strict brute-force rate limiting (5 failed attempts per login token per hour and 10 per IP per 15 minutes); 100% parameterized SQL queries stored in isolated `.sql` files (`backend/queries/`); safe DOM text-node rendering (`document.createTextNode`) and an HTML allowlist sanitizer (`backend/lib/htmlclean.py`) to block cross-site scripting (XSS); strict HTTP security headers (`Content-Security-Policy`, `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`); and mandatory database-level `audit_log` checks on every transaction. Additionally, all adult users (sponsors, chaperones, and board members, who must be 18 or older) authenticate with email two-factor authentication (2FA), and administrative sessions are shortened from 180 days to 72 hours. The Host School Sponsor serves as the designated adult contact overseeing the Written Information Security Program.

### California Data Breach Notification Law *(Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.82)*
California Civil Code § 1798.82 requires organizations to notify affected California residents in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay if unencrypted personal information (a resident's first name or initial and last name paired with covered data elements or account credentials) is acquired by an unauthorized person. Under **Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.82(f)**, if a single breach requires notifying **more than 500 California residents**, the organization must also electronically submit a sample copy of the breach notification notice (excluding personally identifiable information) to the **California Attorney General**.

Our ID-only student database provides structural immunity against statutory student data breaches: even if the entire Turso database were exfiltrated, an attacker would obtain only anonymous ID numbers, grades (6–12), Latin levels, and keyed HMAC digests—zero student names, emails, phones, or addresses, and no access to the sponsors' private Google Spreadsheets. For adult records in the database (roughly 100 to 150 adults statewide, well below the 500-resident threshold) and any restricted Google Drive files, we maintain a four-phase incident response plan (`docs/PRIVACY.md` § 8 and `docs/RUNBOOK.md` § 11): containment and secret rotation within 1 hour, `audit_log` forensic assessment within 24 hours, vulnerability remediation, and written notice to affected individuals, sponsors, and school districts within 30 days—plus electronic notification to the California Attorney General under § 1798.82(f) in the event a breach of non-anonymized records (such as scanned paper waivers in Google Drive) ever affected more than 500 California residents.

### CalOPPA *(Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 22575–22579 — California Online Privacy Protection Act)*
CalOPPA applies to online services that collect personally identifiable contact information (such as a name paired with an email address or phone number) from California residents (§ 22577(a)). Because student records contain zero contact fields, CalOPPA is triggered only by the email addresses and mobile phone numbers collected from adult sponsors and chaperones (who must be 18 or older).

In compliance with CalOPPA, we link our plain-language Privacy Policy (`#/privacy`) conspicuously in the global footer of every page on the website. The policy discloses the exact categories of data collected, our sub-processors, the effective date, our contact information (the Host School Sponsor at `state@uhsjcl.org`), and how we respond to browser "Do Not Track" (DNT) signals—namely, that we perform zero cross-site or behavioral tracking for any visitor regardless of browser settings.

### CCPA / CPRA *(Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.100 et seq. — California Consumer Privacy Act)*
The CCPA and CPRA apply only to for-profit businesses that meet statutory revenue (over $25 million) or data-volume thresholds, and explicitly exclude both 501(c)(3) non-profit organizations and de-identified data (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(d), (m), (v)). CAJCL is therefore doubly exempt from the CCPA/CPRA, while still voluntarily providing parents, students, and sponsors with full rights to inspect, correct, and delete their data and never selling or sharing information.

### California Right of Publicity & Minor Likeness Rules *(Cal. Civ. Code § 3344 & COPPA)*
California Civil Code § 3344 prohibits the knowing use of another person's photograph or likeness on merchandise or for commercial advertising without prior consent, while explicitly exempting non-commercial educational and organizational events (§ 3344(d)). For children under 13, COPPA additionally requires parental consent before collecting identifiable photographs online.

Because CAJCL is a non-profit educational organization running an internal convention photo contest and projecting winning photos at the live General Assembly, there is zero commercial use of any attendee's likeness. To ensure full compliance with COPPA and respect attendee privacy, the physical *Student Waiver & Permission Form* signed by every parent includes an express convention media and photography release, our upload pipeline strips all EXIF/XMP/IPTC/GPS metadata in both the browser and the server (`backend/lib/photos.py`), uploaded files in Google Drive are labeled solely by Chapter and Assigned Delegate ID, only the Activities Chair and Convention Presidents can view submitted contest entries inside the portal, and any photo can be withdrawn by the student or taken down immediately by the chair. All uploaded photos in Google Drive are permanently deleted on **April 12, 2027** (and any rolling Drive backups expire within 10 days; note that if a winning or event photo has already been posted publicly on social media such as Instagram with consent, deleting a record from the website does not automatically delete the Instagram post, though we can take it down upon request).

### Non-Profit Financial Recordkeeping *(Internal Revenue Code § 501(c)(3) & Cal. Corp. Code § 6320)*
As a tax-exempt 501(c)(3) public benefit corporation collecting institutional registration fees, CAJCL is required by federal tax law and California corporate law to maintain accurate financial books and check remittance ledgers for up to seven years for audit purposes. Our financial ledger (`payments` and `schools` tables) is append-only, records only chapter-level institutional check numbers, payment amounts, and aggregate approved headcounts, and contains zero student personally identifiable information. As a result, chapter financial ledgers can be retained for the full seven-year statutory audit window with zero impact on student privacy.
