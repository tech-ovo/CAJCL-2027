## 1. Signing in

Go to [**state.uhsjcl.org**](https://state.uhsjcl.org). No usernames/passwords. Everyone signs in with a permanent code like `XXX-XXXXX-XXXXX`. The first three letters are `SPO` for a sponsor, `VOL` for parents/chaperones, and `DEL` for a delegate. Your codes are on Discord!

Sponsors will give attendees their code on a printed packet. That packet will also have QR codes that attendees can sign. If somebody loses a code, you need to issue a new one ([§9](#9-when-something-goes-wrong)).

## 2. What you see

When you log in, you'll see this menu.

```
Welcome   Registration  |  Overview   Chapters   Check-in   Your name   Sign out
```

The first **Registration** is your own form. Everything else is stuff for your job.

The three after the divider are your job:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | Everything at once. Where you look to answer "how are we doing". |
| **Chapters** | One chapter at a time. Where you go to *do* something. |
| **Check-in** | The Friday desk. Ignore it until March. |

**Sign out is on every page, never in a menu.** Assume you are on a shared
laptop, because at convention you will be.

## 3. Before you open: the chapters exist

Registration cannot open until every chapter has a row on the site and a sponsor
with a code, because the email you are about to send contains that code.

Go to **Chapters → Add a chapter**. You need:

- **Name** — exactly as they will recognise it. "Pinnacle Bay High School", not
  "Pinnacle Bay".
- **City**
- **Level** — MS or HS. A school sending both a middle and a high school
  delegation is **two chapters**, not one, because almost everything downstream
  depends on the level: which Latin levels exist, which tests are open.
- **Discount**, and a reason for it, if one was agreed.
- **This chapter is not billed** — the tick for SCL. Their invoice comes to zero
  and says why. It is a property of the chapter rather than a name we check for,
  so it keeps working however somebody types "SCL".

The new chapter has nobody in it. Open it — **Roster** on its row — and you will
see a notice saying nobody can sign in to it, with **Add the sponsor** beside it.
That asks for a first name, last name and email, creates their account, and
shows you their access code.

**That code is shown once.** Nothing can recover it. Copy it somewhere before
you leave the screen. If you lose it before sending, issue a new one — same two
clicks, no harm done.

Send each code in a message addressed to that sponsor alone. A code in a message
with forty recipients is a code forty people hold.

## 4. Opening registration: the email

This is the one message that opens registration. For most sponsors it is the
only thing they will read before they start. It has four jobs and nothing else:
what to do first, what it costs, which forms are paper, and who to ask.

**Every figure in it comes off the site.** Before you send, have an admin open
**Settings → Values** and read the fee, the deadlines and the dates off the page
rather than out of last year's email. The *additional adult* fee is the one
figure here that appears on no other page, so check it twice.

Send **one message per chapter**, merging two columns: chapter name and access
code.

### The email

> **Subject:** Registration is open — 72nd CAJCL State Convention, March 12–13

Dear Latin teachers and JCL sponsors,

Registration for the **72nd California Junior Classical League State
Convention** is now open. The convention is **March 12–13, 2027**, hosted
jointly by University High School and Woodbridge High School, with everything
taking place on the University High School campus at 4771 Campus Drive, Irvine.

Registration is online this year, at **state.uhsjcl.org**. Your access code is
at the bottom of this email.

**What to do first**

Sign in and paste your roster. You do not need to format it — paste a column
out of a spreadsheet, a numbered list, or one name per line, and it will read
it. You will see exactly what it understood before anything is saved, and you
can correct any row. Most chapters finish this in under five minutes.

Each of your delegates and adults then gets a sheet with their own access code
and a square code they can scan with a phone camera. Hand each sheet to the
person named on it. They sign in and complete their own form: grade, Latin
level, meal preference, and the events they would like to enter.

**None of the event choices are binding.** They tell the Academics, Activities
and Athletics chairs how many students to prepare materials for, and your
delegates can change their answers as often as they like until the deadline.

**What it costs**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Per delegate | $140.00 |
| Adults | one free per 10 delegates |
| Each additional adult | $75.00 |

Your invoice is on the site and updates itself as your roster changes, so check
it there rather than working from a printed copy. Make checks payable to
**University High School JCL c/o Mark Michalak**, and write your chapter name on
the memo line so we can match the payment to your invoice.

**Dates to keep**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Forms close | February 13, 2027 |
| Payment due | February 13, 2027 |
| Convention | March 12–13, 2027 |

After the forms deadline your delegates can no longer edit their own answers.
You can still ask a registration chair to reopen a form.

**Three forms are on paper and are not online**

- **Student Waiver** — every delegate, parent or guardian signature required
- **Student Medical Form** — every delegate, parent or guardian signature required
- **Adult Medical Form** — every adult attending, sponsors included

Collect all three, tick each one off in your roster on the site as it reaches
you, scan the packet into your chapter's Drive folder, and mail the paper with
your check. Signatures and legibility are checked at Friday check-in.

**If something goes wrong**

Reply to this email, or write to **state@uhsjcl.org**. If a delegate loses their
sheet you can issue them a new code yourself from your roster — the old one
stops working immediately.

We are looking forward to seeing your chapter in March.

*[Name]*
*Registration Chair, 72nd CAJCL State Convention*
*state@uhsjcl.org*

---

**Your chapter:** [Chapter name]
**Your access code:** `SPO-XXXXX-XXXXX`

Keep this code. Anyone who has it can sign in as you and see your whole chapter.
If you lose it, write to state@uhsjcl.org and we will issue another.

### Before you send it

- [ ] The fee, the deadlines and the dates match **Settings → Values**
- [ ] The remit-to name is right
- [ ] Every sponsor is on the site with a code
- [ ] The site is up and answers at the address in the email
- [ ] Somebody who is not you has read it and can say what to do first

## 5. Watching it come in

**Overview** is the page you will live on. Nothing on it needs a refresh button;
every number was counted at the moment the thing it counts changed.

**The five figures across the top**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Chapters | How many, and how many have anybody on their roster yet |
| Delegates | |
| Adults | Split into sponsors and chaperones |
| Forms complete | Out of everyone attending |
| Still owed | And how many chapters have settled |

*Complete* means a specific thing, and it is worth knowing exactly:

- A **delegate** is complete when they have submitted their own form online
  **and** their waiver and medical form have reached their sponsor, who has
  ticked both.
- An **adult** is complete when they have submitted their form and their medical
  form has been ticked.

So a chapter stuck at 12/30 is usually waiting on paper, not on the website.

**Meals** has its own block because it answers somebody else's question on
somebody else's deadline. The caterer wants a number; the "still to come" figure
is the part you chase. It counts everybody attending — chapters and SCL alike —
and only people who are still coming. Somebody who withdrew is not eating.

**Chapters**, the table underneath, is every chapter with a row you can click
through to. It does not list SCL, because there is no sponsor to chase there and
no invoice to settle — but SCL is in the totals above and at check-in, because
they are attending.

## 6. Chasing the chapters that stall

Tick **Only chapters needing attention** on Overview.

That filter is deliberately narrow. It shows three things, and each one is a
phone call somebody can act on today:

- **Nobody on the roster.** They have not started.
- **No sponsor.** Nobody can sign in at all. Fix it yourself —
  [§3](#3-before-you-open-the-chapters-exist).
- **Money outstanding**, on a chapter that is actually billed.

A chapter that is simply not finished yet is none of those, and burying the
urgent ones among thirty half-done ones would make the filter useless.

About two weeks after the email, the chapters with nothing on their roster are
the ones to write to. That is a short second message, not a repeat of the first.

## 7. Money

There are **no refunds**, and that one policy explains everything else you will
see.

Go to **Chapters**. Every chapter shows what it owes, what it has paid, and the
balance. Press **Payment** on a chapter's row and enter:

- **Amount** in dollars
- **Check number**
- **Date received**
- **Note**, if anything about it needs explaining

Payments are only ever added, never edited or deleted. If you enter $1,400 and
it should have been $140, you do not go back and fix it — **you add a second
payment of −$1,260**. The balance is always the sum of everything recorded, and
the trail shows what actually happened and when.

**The invoice recalculates itself.** Adding a delegate changes what a chapter
owes the moment the roster changes. Never work from a printed copy, or from a
figure somebody read to you last week.

**Somebody who withdraws after their chapter paid still counts.** There are no
refunds, so they stay on the invoice. Somebody who withdraws before the chapter
paid comes off it. The site tracks the difference; you do not have to.

**An exempt chapter shows "Not billed"** rather than $0.00, so nobody wonders
whether the zero is a mistake.

## 8. Inside one chapter

From **Chapters → Roster**, or by clicking a chapter's name on Overview.

You see who is in it, what each person is, and their number — the same number
printed on their sheet, so a sponsor reading one out over the phone is reading
the same thing you are looking at.

**Position** is more useful than "Adult" repeated down a column. It shows
Sponsor, Chaperone, SCL — or a board title, because a convention president is a
delegate and "Delegate" is true but not what you need to see.

What you can do here:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **New code** | On one person's row. Their old code stops working at once. |
| **Issue new codes** | The same for several people. Tick them, then confirm. |
| **Cancel** | Takes somebody off the roster. Reversible — **Restore** brings them back. |
| **Make leader** | Marks a delegate as chapter leader, which lets them enter their chapter's team events. |
| **Preview packet** | The printed sheets, as a preview. |
| **Show 3 cancelled** | Counts them in the button, and only appears when there are any. |

**Preview packet is a preview, not something to hand out.** Because codes are
stored scrambled, the preview shows blocks where the codes would be. To produce
a sheet somebody can actually use, issue them a new code — the screen that
follows has the print link for exactly those sheets.

**You will not see grades, Latin levels, or the paper-form ticks here.** Those
are the sponsor's working state and there is nothing you could do about any of
them from this page. The sponsor sees all of it on their own roster.

## 9. When something goes wrong

### "I lost my code" / "my delegate lost their sheet"

The sponsor can do this themselves, and it is worth telling them so — it is one
button on their own roster. If they would rather you did it: open the chapter's
roster, find the person, press **New code**.

The new code appears once, with a link to reprint that person's sheet. Print it
before you leave the screen. The old code stops working immediately, and any
device signed in with it is signed out.

If a whole chapter has lost their sheets — it happens; sheets get printed and
then left in a classroom — use **Issue new codes**, tick everybody, and print
the lot in one go.

### "The site says my code is wrong and it isn't"

Nearly always one of:

- **The code was reissued** and they are holding the old sheet. This is the
  common one.
- **Too many tries.** Five wrong attempts on one code in an hour pauses it.
  Wait, then try again.
- **A genuine mis-read.** Codes never contain the letters `I`, `L`, `O` or `Z`,
  so those cannot be confused with `1`, `0` and `2` — and if somebody types `O`
  for a zero anyway, the site quietly reads it as a zero. Capitals, lower case,
  extra spaces and missing dashes are all fine too. What it will not forgive is
  a character genuinely read wrong, which is the point: every code carries a
  check character, so a single wrong letter is caught rather than signing
  somebody into the wrong account.

### "I need to change a delegate's name"

The sponsor can, from their roster. You cannot, from yours — see
[§11](#11-what-you-cannot-do).

### "My delegate submitted their form with the wrong Latin level"

Ask an admin. Reopening a submitted form is something the site can do and has no
button for yet.

### "The deadline passed and we still need to change something"

Same answer. The deadline stops delegates editing their own answers; it does not
stop a chair.

### "A chapter paid the wrong amount"

Add a second payment for the difference, positive or negative. Never edit the
first. [§7](#7-money).

### "Somebody dropped out"

**Cancel** them on the roster. If their chapter has already paid they stay on
the invoice — there are no refunds — but they come out of the meal count,
because they are not eating.

### "A chapter says their invoice is wrong"

Look at it with them on the site rather than at whatever they printed. Almost
every disagreement is a roster that changed after they printed it. If it still
looks wrong, the free-adult ratio is usually the surprise: one adult free per
ten delegates, so the eleventh delegate makes a second adult free.

### "The page is spinning / says the server is not responding"

The site sleeps when nobody is using it and takes a few seconds to wake. It says
it is waking rather than pretending to be fast. If it lasts more than about
thirty seconds, tell an admin.

### "Somebody registered who should not have"

Cancel them. Every change on the site is recorded with who made it and when, in
the same breath as the change itself, so there is nothing you need to write down
separately.

## 10. The Friday desk

**Check-in** is built for standing up, on a phone, with a queue in front of you
and venue wifi doing what venue wifi does.

Chapters arrive after school, roughly fifty of them in about ninety minutes.
They arrive **together, in a bus**, which is why check-in is per chapter and not
per person. Ticking sixty individual boxes with a queue behind you is not a
thing anybody would do — and the per-person question that matters, whether their
waiver and medical arrived, was answered weeks ago when the paper reached their
sponsor.

**Chapters that have not arrived are listed first**, because you work down what
is left. There is a search box; it filters as you type without waiting for the
network.

Tap anywhere on a chapter's row. That opens a panel with:

- **Notes.** A real box, not a two-inch slot. Anything the desk needs to
  remember: how many Certamen machines, whether they brought a catapult, who is
  missing, what they are waiting on.
- **Registration complete** — marks them arrived, with the time.
- **Unregister** — un-marks them.

**Two buttons rather than one switch, on purpose.** Each says what it does
rather than flipping whatever the last state was, so pressing it twice is the
same as pressing it once. At a desk that is the difference between a queue and a
problem.

The time recorded is the site's, not your phone's, so a phone with a wrong clock
cannot write a check-in time nobody can reconcile.

SCL appears here even though it is not a chapter, because they turn up too.

Notes are per chapter, not per person. A note about one delegate is really a
note about the chapter that brought them, and splitting it would mean deciding
whose row a catapult goes in.

## 11. What you cannot do

Some of this is deliberate. Some is simply not built yet, and knowing which is
which saves you looking for a button that is not there.

**Deliberate**

- **Sign in as somebody else.** Only a convention president can, and it costs
  them their own code typed again. A walked-away laptop should not be one click
  from reading a chapter's roster as its sponsor.
- **Change the fee, the deadlines, or any wording on the site.** That is
  Settings, and it belongs to the presidents.
- **Read the audit log.** It records everything you do; reading it is a
  president's job.
- **Touch medical or waiver documents.** They never come near this system. They
  are paper, scanned by sponsors into a Drive folder that no part of this site
  reads.
- **Collect delegate email addresses.** They are never asked for. Several
  delegates are eleven years old.

**Not built yet — ask an admin, and tell us if you need it often**

- **Pasting a roster on a chapter's behalf.** If a sponsor cannot get their
  spreadsheet in, an admin can do it as them.
- **Adding one person to a roster.** Sponsors add people by pasting again;
  a second paste adds to the roster rather than replacing it.
- **Reopening a submitted form.**
- **Correcting a chapter's name, city or discount** after it is created.
- **Waiving the activity sheet** for somebody added at the desk on the Friday.

Every one of these already works inside the site; each is missing only a button.
If one turns out to be something you need weekly rather than twice, say so and
it moves up the list.
